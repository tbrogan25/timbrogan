# How to Validate

Validating is a simple task with a lot of nuance. Let's go over all the nuance here with some examples.

When we refer to a "signature," we are referring to the entire row of one person's handwritten data, not just the actual signature.

### Search by address

To be as quick as possible, search by address and municipality first. They are likely more legible than the handwritten name.

Correctly searching for an address and municipality yields all residents at the address.

The AI result for the address and municipality will automatically be searched in the searchbox in an attempt to speed up the process for you. But sometimes, it misreads the address and/or municipality, meaning you have to edit it and search again.

### Common examples

To mark a signature with a messy address or name as valid, you need to confidently make out enough "clues" from the written name or signature that match a resident at the address.

This could include the first and last initials, parts of the first or last name, or combinations of everything. This only works if the clues match _only one resident_ at the address. If there are multiple residents at an address that match the clues in a signature and you cannot confidently determine that it's a singular resident, then it cannot be marked valid.

#### What if the address isn't coming up?

If no address comes up, it either means no one is registered at that address, which is rare, or something is off.

Make sure you're typing the street name and municipality as accurately as possible.

The following address is "30 Quails Crossing, Marion" but no results for that address appear.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (83).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

Troubleshoot by adding the name to the search:

"danae ann 30 quails crossing marion"

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (85).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

It appears that "Crossing" is formatted as "Xing" in the database, causing the issue.

Don't give up on addresses that don't appear immediately. Troubleshoot by making sure the address and municipality are correct adding the name of the signer to the front.&#x20;

Use Google Maps to help you. Have Google Maps open on another tab and copy the address there.

#### Nicknames

People won't be registered under a nickname or shortened version of their name, even if they write it on the sheet. When you see a common nickname, try to infer the full name.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (51).png" alt=""><figcaption><p>"Jen Hall" appears as "Jennifer Hall" in the database</p></figcaption></figure>

Even if someone writes a nickname, it is valid, as long as the nickname is a common nickname for their full registered name.

#### Use street suffix abbreviations

The suffixes of street names ("lane," "circle," "street," "avenue," "terrace," etc.) are abbreviated in the database. Abbreviate them while searching to increase the chance of finding a match ("ln," "cir," "st," "ave," "ter," etc.).

#### Spell out the number in street names that are numbers

Sometimes if a street name is a number, spell it out. They can only be found in the database if they're fully spelt out.

#### Use Google Maps to your advantage

We always keep Google Maps open in another tab. This is because we can use it to check if an address is real or not.

If we think we're misreading an address due to messy handwriting, we can type the address into Google Maps and see if it exists. If nothing appears, then we know we're reading the address wrong. Sometimes it can even autocorrect the name.

### Marking a signature invalid

If you can't find a resident in the database or if you can't read the handwriting, you need to mark the signature as invalid.

You will choose one of three rejection reasons:

* **Illegible** – handwriting is so unreadable you cannot even make a genuine search attempt for the name or address.
* **Failed Match** – name and address are readable enough to make a genuine search attempt for the name or address, but no match appears.

To actually physically mark a signature as invalid, you need to press the corresponding key on the keyboard. You also need to be selecting the signature image box itself to press these keys because otherwise, you would just be typing in the search box.&#x20;

You will be highlighting the search box by default. To highlight the signature image box, click on it so your cursor is removed from the searchbox.

#### No Street Number

If a signer just puts their street name with no number, that is automatically a **Failed Match**.

This is because they haven't properly identified that they live at a given address. Any choice you make would be an assumption.&#x20;

If a signer puts the wrong street number, this is also automatically a **Failed Match**.

#### Wrong Municipality

You would also mark a signature as a **Failed Match** if the signer is from the wrong municipality. Only signers who are registered in the municipality at the bottom of the sheet can sign that sheet, or else you need to mark them invalid.

The example below shows a signer that's registered in Hancock who signed a sheet for Pittsfield.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (56).png" alt=""><figcaption><p>Voter from Hancock signed on a sheet for Pittsfield</p></figcaption></figure>

If there is no municipality written on the bottom of the sheet, that is acceptable.

#### Different Last Name

Even if a result is most likely the signer, if the last name is different, it is a **Failed Match**.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (57).png" alt=""><figcaption><p>Tannia Bolton at 6 Louise St is probably Tannia Zradi at 6 Louise St, but it is still invalid</p></figcaption></figure>

#### Two People with the Same Name at the Same Address

It's common to see two people with the same name at an address.

Only mark one of them valid if you can tell which person it is, usually by a differentiating middle initial or by checking their birthdays by clicking the drop-down arrow on the right if the signer has written "Jr." or "Sr."

#### Deleting a signature

Sometimes, people cross out their information after signing a sheet. Sometimes, our AI does not detect if a signature is crossed out and still presents it to you for validation. Press the red trashcan icon to delete these types of signatures.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (17) (1).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### Undoing a Signature

If you accidentally validated a signature that should not have been valid or you made the wrong decision, press the Undo button to have a fresh start. Be wary, however, that you can only undo your decision for the last signature you validated. You cannot undo the decisions you made for any signatures earlier than the one you just validated.

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (96).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### Villages

In Massachusetts, there are many "villages" within towns. For example, the town of Billerica, MA, has a village called Pinehurst. In some instances, a sheet will have "Pinehurst" written at the bottom of it where the municipality should go. Pinehurst will not show up in the searchbox because it is not a real town.

If you suspect a town is really a village, then look at the following link for a list of all municipalities in the state. If it is not in this list, then it is likely a village. Do a quick Google search to see what town your village belongs to.

\
[https://www.mass.gov/lists/massachusetts-city-and-town-websites](https://www.mass.gov/lists/massachusetts-city-and-town-websites)

#### Bugs to Watch Out For

_Misaligned Rows_

Sometimes signers will skip rows in between their signatures, leaving blank rows in between. This messes up the calibration of the image-splicing mechanism. After seeing a blank row, any remaining signature images on the sheet will be slightly off center. Please refer to the attempted AI translation above the image to identify what row you're working on, then look at the entire sheet image on the right to see the full signature.&#x20;

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (68).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

<figure><img src=".gitbook/assets/image (69).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

_Accidental Validation_

If you have a voter selected from the results (highlighted with blue) but you go back to the searchbox to search for a different voter, you will end up accidentally validating the selected voter even though you're still in the textbox. Avoid selecting a voter before making a decision.

_Inaccurate Progress Bar_

Sometimes you will experience a bug with the progress bar at the top where it will have percentage values over 100% or say that you have less than 0 signatures remaining. Just ignore this.
