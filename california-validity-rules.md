# California Validity Rules

For California, every signature must be categorized as **Good**, **Bad**, or **Failed**.

These categories are **not about intent** and **not about how real a person seems**. They are about whether the signature is acceptable under California rules and whether it matches the voter database in an allowed way.

***

### 🟢 Good

A **Good** signature is acceptable and can be matched to a specific voter record, even if some information is imperfect. California allows a reasonable amount of flexibility, as long as the core identifying information matches and the rules below are followed.

A Good signature does not need to be perfect — it needs to be acceptable.

***

### 🟠 Bad

A **Bad** signature is complete and readable, but it cannot be accepted because the information does not match the voter file in a way that California allows.

All fields are filled out, and the handwriting is clear, but the mismatch is significant enough that the signature cannot be counted.

***

### 🔴 Failed

A **Failed** signature is structurally unacceptable, regardless of whether the signer might be a real voter.

Once a signature is Failed, no further matching should be attempted.

***

### What Makes a Signature **Good**

A signature should be marked **Good** when it matches a voter record and stays within California’s allowed flexibility.

#### City and ZIP Rules

California allows **either** the city **or** the ZIP code to be incorrect — but **not both**.

* City incorrect, ZIP correct → **Good**
* ZIP incorrect, city correct → **Good**
* City incorrect **and** ZIP incorrect → **Bad**

#### Name Rules

**First Names**

* First names may be shortened or initialized.
* For example, _Derek_ may appear as _Der_ or _D_ and still be acceptable.

**Last Names**

* Last names must **not** be shortened, abbreviated, or initialized.
* The written last name must match the voter file.

**Double Last Names**

* If a voter has two last names (hyphenated or compound), the signer may write **either one**.
* Writing at least one of the two last names is sufficient for a Good signature.

{% hint style="info" %}
John Doe-Smith signing as either _John Doe_ or _John Smith_ is considered a Good signature.
{% endhint %}

#### Address Rules

When reviewing an address, focus only on the **street number and street name**.

* Apartment/unit numbers, house fraction numbers (10 ½ Main St), address number suffixes (10A Main St), or street direction prefixes (e.g., _West_, _N_, _S_). should be ignored entirely. If they are different than in the voter database or simply missing, the signature is still Good.
* Street names may be shortened or abbreviated, as long as the intent is clear.

For multi-word street names:

* Writing part of the name or shortening the words is acceptable.
* Turning the entire street name into initials or a vague acronym is **not acceptable**\
  (e.g., _Rocky Sands Blvd → RS Blvd_).&#x20;

#### City Field (Special Rule)

California is very lenient with city names.

* Abbreviations are acceptable (e.g., _LB_ for _Long Beach_).
* For multi-word city names, at least **one letter per word** is sufficient.
* The city field is treated more flexibly than the street name field.

***

### What Makes a Signature **Bad**

A signature should be marked **Bad** when all fields are filled out and readable, but the signature cannot be validated.

Common Bad scenarios include:

* No voter appears in the search results.
* A likely voter appears, but:
  * The written address does not match the voter file.
  * The last name differs due to a name change, such as marriage.
  * Both the city **and** ZIP code are incorrect.

Bad signatures are complete — they simply do not meet California's acceptance criteria.

***

### What Makes a Signature **Failed**

A signature should be marked **Failed** when it does not meet minimum structural requirements.

Mark a signature **Failed** if **any** of the following apply:

* The signer is not registered in the county printed on the petition sheet.
* A PO Box or other non-address information is used in the address field.
* One or more required fields are blank.
* Names or fields are crossed out by the signer.
* The last name is abbreviated, shortened, or initialized.
* There is no street number.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Some Failed signatures may belong to real voters. This does not matter.
{% endhint %}
